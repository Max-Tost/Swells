# Swells

How a storm at sea becomes sets of waves at a beach — derived, coded, and
simulated.

A low sits in the Southern Ocean for two days and builds sixteen-metre chaos
with no discernible period. Six days and nine thousand kilometres later, a reef
in Indonesia receives clean twenty-second lines, three metres, arriving
three at a time with seven minutes of nothing in between.

This works out why, from the equations of motion to the breaking wave.

## What is here

**`lessons/`** — twelve markdown lessons that derive the physics. Assumes
calculus and Fourier analysis; assumes nothing about fluid mechanics or
oceanography.

**`swells/`** — the same physics as a Python package. Every equation in the
lessons is a named function here, with the same symbols.

**`app/`** — a browser simulator. Set the storm, the distance and the beach;
watch the swell arrive, group into sets, shoal and break.

**`figures/`** — every figure in the lessons, generated by script.

**`tests/`** — 78 tests, mostly analytic limits, conservation laws, and
independent cross-checks rather than regression snapshots.

## Running it

```bash
uv venv && uv pip install numpy scipy matplotlib pytest

.venv/bin/python -m pytest tests/ -q      # 78 passed
.venv/bin/python figures/make_figures.py  # regenerate the figures
.venv/bin/python app/serve.py             # then open http://localhost:8000/app/
```

From Python:

```python
from swells import Storm, Coast, run, surf_report

print(surf_report(run(
    storm=Storm(U10=30, fetch_km=1200, duration_h=48, distance_km=9000),
    coast=Coast(slope=0.04, approach_deg=20))))
```

## Two things worth knowing

**The lessons and the code are one artifact.** Every derived equation is a
named function, every figure comes from a committed script, and the browser app
runs the actual package through Pyodide rather than a JavaScript port. There is
one copy of the physics.

**JONSWAP contradicts itself.** The $\alpha$ and $f_p$ scaling laws imply
$H_s\propto\tilde x^{0.55}$; the measured energy law says $\tilde x^{0.50}$. At
ordinary storm fetch the two differ by 24%. We side with the energy law and
rescale, `calibrate=False` shows the raw discrepancy, and
`test_the_two_jonswap_fits_disagree_and_we_know_by_how_much` pins the size of
it. See lesson 4.

## Sources

Stokes (1880) on the limiting wave. Miles (1957) and Phillips (1957) on wind
input. Hasselmann (1962) on four-wave interaction. Pierson & Moskowitz (1964)
and Hasselmann et al. (1973) on spectra. Longuet-Higgins & Stewart (1964) on

groups. Battjes & Janssen (1978) and Goda (2010) on breaking. Ardhuin et al. on
swell dissipation. Textbooks: Holthuijsen, *Waves in Oceanic and Coastal
Waters*; Dean & Dalrymple, *Water Wave Mechanics*; Mei, *Applied Dynamics of
